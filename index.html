Methodology

  • Total cost basis = the actual amount the wallet committed, valued on deposit day — what went in before the protocol converted it into the pool position. The purest comparable figure: Ryze pools aren't 50/50, so the converted split differs with every deposit. Capital at work time-weights that over the window (a deposit made later counts for fewer days), which is why it can read lower than total cost basis. The APR is always figured on capital at work.
  • Capital at work = FIFO cost basis of what's in the pool, averaged over the window: each deposit is valued at that day's close (Kraken daily closes, USDC = $1); withdrawals consume the oldest lots first at their original cost. Market moves after a deposit never change it.
  • Current value = your live staked position, read on-chain from each pool's staking gauge via getStake(address) and priced as pool reserves ÷ LP supply — the exact position right now, not an estimate. If the on-chain read fails, it falls back to valuing your remaining deposit lots at today's closes, marked (est.). The gap between current value and capital at work is market gain or loss.
  • Deposits: transactions where the wallet sends USDC/WETH/cbBTC and the same transaction deposits LP into a Ryze pool. Plain swaps excluded; dust returned to the wallet is netted off.
  • Withdrawals: transactions that burn pool LP with underlying returned to the wallet.
  • Rewards = claimed (pool → wallet USDC transfers) + unclaimed (read live from each pool's claimable-rewards view).
  • Realized APR = rewards ÷ capital at work × 365/days (capital at work is the time-weighted average over the window; total cost basis is shown alongside so you can see what actually went in). APY = (1+APR/52)52−1 (weekly compounding).
How capital at work is computed, step by step
  1. Find the flows. Every deposit and withdrawal becomes a dated entry: date, pool, and token amounts (USDC / WETH / cbBTC). Claims never touch capital at work.
  2. Pool migrations net out. When a withdrawal is followed within 2 days by deposits into the same pool (e.g. a pool migration: withdraw from the old pool, deposit into the new one), the pair is netted to its capital delta — only genuinely new money counts as a deposit.
  3. Build FIFO lots, day by day. Starting from the first activity day through today, each day's deposits push a cost-basis lot valued at that day's close (USDC at $1, WETH at the ETH close, cbBTC at the BTC close — Kraken daily closes, Coinbase Exchange fallback). Each day's withdrawals consume the oldest lots first at their original cost. Cost basis can never go negative.
  4. Time-weight it. Capital at work is the average of the daily cost-basis balances, per pool. The blended figure is the sum of both pools. Because each lot carries its deposit-day price, market moves after a flow never change it — so the APR is pure yield, not price appreciation.
  5. Divide rewards by capital at work. Realized APR = total rewards ÷ capital at work × 365 ÷ days in the window.

Limits, stated plainly: capital at work is reconstructed from funds the wallet itself sends — deposits funded directly by a third party can't be attributed from transfer history alone. The figure is rewards-only: it measures reward payouts against capital at work, not total position P&L (no impermanent loss, no in-pool fee accrual).
